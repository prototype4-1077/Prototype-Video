We call ordinary consciousness clear because it is the only clarity we remember using. The world appears direct: light enters, sound arrives, objects sit obediently where we left them. But perception may be less like a window and more like a survival dashboard. A dashboard does not show the entire engine. It shows the few signals needed to keep driving. Your senses discard almost everything, then present the remainder as reality. That is not a flaw. A map survives by refusing to become the territory. DMT may loosen that compression, but loosened does not automatically mean more truthful. A broken radio can reveal hidden frequencies and still distort the music. The profound possibility is not that the filter imprisons us. It may protect us. Imagine reality as white light too intense for a finite nervous system to hold. The mind becomes stained glass, dividing the unbearable whole into colors a human life can touch. Space becomes distance. Time becomes sequence. The ocean of information becomes one cup on one table. Then the filter opens wider, and categories begin flooding into one another. A leaf becomes architecture. Sound acquires weight. Thoughts arrive wearing landscapes. Information stops behaving like a message and becomes weather surrounding the listener. The entities may be meaning compressed into faces because faces are how human minds negotiate importance. The sacred feeling may be infinity passing through machinery designed for breakfast, traffic, and remembering passwords. We often resent our limitations because we imagine boundlessness would feel like freedom. But an eye without an aperture receives so much light that it loses the picture. A photograph needs darkness around each exposure. A song needs silence between its notes. Perhaps the ego is not merely a prison. Perhaps it is the small opening that allows anything coherent to appear. When ordinary perception returns, the spoon is only a spoon again, and this can feel like exile. Yet maybe the ordinary world is an astonishing translation, not a diminished one. What if reality is filtered not to hide infinity from you, but to let you love one piece of it at a time?
