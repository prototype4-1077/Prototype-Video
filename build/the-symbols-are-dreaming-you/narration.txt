You think symbols are things you look at. What if your life is a symbol looking back? A red light means stop. A ring means forever. A hospital bracelet can make one afternoon heavier than a year. Chapter Eighteen of Seth Speaks suggests consciousness is always translating itself into symbols. Raw experience may be too large to enter the room without changing clothes. Consciousness changes focus, giving the same world a different costume. Joy does not visit a room. It secretly repaints it while you stand inside. Depression can drain color from the same walls and convince you the gray was always there. The room did not become your emotion. Emotion became the lens through which it arrived. Objects may be the skin of inner events: a house as safety, a locked drawer as fear, a window as permission. A key can be metal in your hand and possibility in your mind. A staircase can be architecture by day and becoming by dream. Fire can mean danger, desire, purification, home, or the sentence your anger could not pronounce. The symbol is a ferry carrying feeling where ordinary language cannot build a bridge. Everyone keeps a private symbolic bank, filled with deposits nobody else can spend. A stranger sees an old coat. You feel your father's hand resting on your shoulder. A bell rings for everyone. Only you are suddenly eight years old again. In deeper states, symbols loosen. One image may become a funeral, a wedding, a doorway, and sunrise before it stops turning. Dreams feel irrational because they speak several meanings at once, while waking thought insists on taking attendance. Even the blank spaces matter. A pause in consciousness may be a language changing alphabets. What feels like nothing may be the workshop where the next symbol receives a handle. The danger begins when we mistake the ferry for the shore and the symbol for the truth it carries. Religions, paintings, dreams, and bodies may be different alphabets attempting the same impossible sentence. Meaning is not trapped inside them. Meaning uses them, then keeps moving. Perhaps you are also a symbol: a temporary, breathing image through which a larger self is trying to recognize itself. Look around your room. What ordinary object has been quietly carrying a part of you that words could never lift?
