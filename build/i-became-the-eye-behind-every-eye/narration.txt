The room did not disappear. It inhaled. The walls breathed, and objects leaned toward me as if reality had decided to speak. Behind the paint lived a stained-glass lattice, intricate as a thought examining itself. Then my body became transparent. Memories moved through me like colored fish beneath ice. My name slid away like a label falling from an ancient bottle. Without my name, I expected nothingness. Instead, an eye opened where I had been. It was not looking at the universe. The universe was looking through it. I became a stranger on a train, carrying grief behind an ordinary face. I became a child discovering rain, an old woman forgetting home, and a bird turning sunlight into direction. Each life arrived whole, a universe insisting with absolute sincerity that it was alone. Awareness moved through them like white light through stained glass, becoming every color yet belonging to none. Then I saw an endless amphitheater. Billions of eyes filled its seats, each one watching from center stage. There was no audience or performer. Only seeing, wearing distance as a magnificent disguise. For one unbearable, beautiful instant, I was the eye behind every eye. But the experience did not tell me that differences were meaningless. It showed me why they mattered. A window does not betray the light by giving it a particular view. Pain was still pain. A boundary was sacred. No revelation gave cruelty permission to call itself unity. Then I entered a hall of mirrors where every reflection turned away before I could see its face. I understood: the witness cannot stand in front of itself. It can only become another view. Trying to find the final observer was like asking a flashlight to illuminate the source of its own beam. Perhaps I was never a prisoner behind my eyes. I was an aperture through which existence became personal. Every argument became light disagreeing with itself after passing through different prisms. Compassion was not pity. It was the nervous system of existence recognizing one of its distant signals. When I returned, my body felt heavy and astonishing. Even my hands seemed like ancient instruments I had borrowed. Outside, strangers passed beneath streetlights. For a moment, galaxies flickered behind every blink. I no longer wondered whether I was alone. I wondered whether solitude was the universe closing one eyelid so it could dream through the other.
