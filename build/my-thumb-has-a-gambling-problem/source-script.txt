I picked up my phone to check the weather. Forty-seven minutes later, I knew a stranger's divorce strategy, air-fryer recipes, and why Rome collapsed. I still did not know the weather. The sky was not optimized for engagement. This is not a phone. It is a tiny casino that can also call my dentist. No clocks. No windows. Every machine pays in feelings, and the carpet is my personality. My thumb has become a gambler wearing a tiny visor. One more scroll. This next one could be the big one. The big one, for the record, is usually a man pointing at text while standing in a kitchen. The algorithm knows me like a psychic who has been reading my garbage. It watched me pause on one video about sourdough. Now it thinks I abandoned my family to become bread. Every clip arrives like a waiter, lifting a silver lid and whispering, 'Perhaps the gentleman would enjoy some fresh anxiety?' I am not choosing content. Content is auditioning for my nervous system. Outrage brings drums. Nostalgia brings a fog machine. A productivity expert breaks in to explain why resting is inefficient. My brain whispers, 'I'm bored.' My phone hears, 'Deploy the glitter.' I can tolerate four seconds of silence before behaving as though I have been buried alive. Silence knocks. I turn off the lights and pretend nobody is home. The funniest part is that the phone never asks whether I am enjoying myself. It only asks whether I am still there. Then it thoughtfully removes the question. Scrolling is a treadmill made of trapdoors. Every step drops me into the same room with different wallpaper and a person yelling, 'Wait until the end.' I keep searching for the final satisfying post, the one that will tuck my brain into bed and kiss its forehead. There is no final post. That would be terrible casino design. This does not mean I am weak. I am holding an industrial attention harvester while wearing pajamas. Honestly, I am doing excellent work under hostile agricultural conditions. But I am not helpless. I can notice the tiny moment before my hand reaches for the glowing rectangle. I can put it across the room like a misbehaving wizard whose powers depend entirely on Wi-Fi. If the world needs me, it can knock. My thumb is retiring before it qualifies for a pension.
