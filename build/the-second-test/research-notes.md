# The Second Test — source and concept notes

The video's proposed lens asks what an intelligent system makes possible beyond itself. This is a philosophical synthesis, not a claim that intelligence has been newly defined or that nobody has discussed adjacent ideas.

The working definition remains plural: learning, reasoning and generalizing to unfamiliar tasks are useful dimensions of intelligence. The proposed second test measures consequences for the person using AI; it does not replace capability tests or establish consciousness.

The workshop comparison is an imagined metaphor. Different tasks call for different support: getting a finished result can be an entirely legitimate goal. The video does not claim that AI use inevitably weakens people or that every tool must teach.

Research references

1. François Chollet (2019), On the Measure of Intelligence. https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547 — distinguishes attained task skill from efficient acquisition of new skills and discusses the difficulty of defining and measuring intelligence.
2. Hamsa Bastani et al. (2025), Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2422633122. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560616/ — the empirical basis for narration beats 9–11. AI improved supported practice performance, while the unrestricted condition led to poorer subsequent unassisted performance than the no-AI group; learning-oriented guardrails largely mitigated that loss. This does not establish a universal effect for all AI tools, people or tasks.
3. Published correction, doi:10.1073/pnas.2518204122. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403119/ — corrects an author affiliation; it does not change the reported findings.
