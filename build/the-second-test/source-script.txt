An AI can finish your sentence. Can it change what you're able to notice afterward? A stranger intelligence test than another question we already know how to grade. Learning. Reasoning. Handling the unfamiliar. Useful clues to intelligence; there isn't one universally accepted definition. Picture two workshops. Same tools, same unfinished chair. You arrive with shaky hands. In the first, a machine builds it while you watch. Perfect joints. You're impressed. In the second, it helps you discover why wood splits against the grain. Both produce a chair. One also changes what you can do tomorrow. Here's my proposed second test: what does an intelligence make possible beyond itself? One mathematics study found that unrestricted AI help improved students' practice performance. After access ended, those students performed worse independently than students who'd practiced without it. A tutor designed to safeguard learning largely avoided that loss. Design changed the outcome. A better chair doesn't automatically mean a more capable carpenter. Sometimes you just need the chair. Your washing machine can keep its lecture on personal growth. But when understanding matters, the hidden question changes: did the task improve, or did your grip? Maybe intelligence has a footprint beyond the system displaying it. A capacity left in someone else. A sharper question. A mistake you can finally recognize. A method you can carry away. That's an effect worth measuring. It doesn't establish consciousness or replace tests of the machine's abilities. And help can remain available. Instructions, tools, other people: your workshop has always contained more than you. Can you inspect the joint, question the plan, and choose what you're building? Back in this room, feel the table beneath your hands. Someone learned to make that. You can borrow a result or gain a possibility. Different needs, different kinds of help. An answer closes a task. A good encounter might open a capacity. That's the second test. The page is finished. The pencil is still in your hand. After the answer... what can you see that you couldn't see before?
