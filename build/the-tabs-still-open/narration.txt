You may not be tired from doing. You may be tired from everything your mind refuses to close. Your brain can look perfectly still while running forty-seven invisible tabs behind your eyes. One is replaying a conversation. Another is rehearsing a future that has not agreed to happen. Three are comparing your life to people whose tabs you cannot even see. Somewhere, music is playing, but you cannot find which page started it. That is mental fatigue: not always one enormous burden, but dozens of tiny processes sipping power. The unfinished message. The waiting decision. The favor owed. The version of you expected tomorrow. None looks heavy alone. Together, they turn your inner browser into a space heater. Then you sit down to rest, but the tabs keep refreshing themselves. Your body is on the couch. Your attention is answering imaginary emails in six different futures. And every tab believes it is urgent. Naturally. Anxiety has never been known for respecting office hours. So you solve everything before resting, as if peace waited at the internet’s bottom. But some tabs are not problems. They are questions with no answer available today. Some belong to an older version of you who forgot to log out. Some are windows you keep open because closing them feels like admitting the story ended. Here is the turn: closure is not always finding the answer. Sometimes closure is stopping the search. You can close a page without declaring it meaningless, defeated, forgiven, or finished forever. You are only deciding it does not get to consume memory tonight. Let the hidden question remain hidden until you actually have the strength to ask it. Look around this room. Name three objects that are not asking anything from you. Feel your feet. Take one slow breath. Notice the world continuing without a status update. Imagine a task manager inside you. Which process is using power without producing anything new? Maybe you do not need a better answer tonight—just fewer questions running at once. Which tab could you close—not solve, not erase, just close—until you choose to open it again?
