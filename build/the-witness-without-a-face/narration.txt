People describe DMT as leaving the body. The stranger possibility is that you leave the person. At first, the familiar pieces loosen: your name, your age, the room, the reason you came. We usually imagine those things are masks, and beneath them waits a truer face. But what if the final layer is not another face? Your biography disappears first, like a passport dropped into deep water. Then the body becomes a rumor being repeated somewhere very far away. Time loses its sequence. Before and after spill from their containers and mix on the floor. And still, somehow, the disappearance is being noticed. That is the impossible little ember at the center of the experience. Something witnesses the self vanish, but it cannot introduce itself without building a new self. Ask the witness its name, and you have already handed it a mask. It is like a flashlight trying to turn around and illuminate the bulb producing its light. Or a mirror searching its own surface for the reflection that makes reflection possible. Imagine a house going dark room by room: childhood, ambition, fear, even the room marked me. At the end, one open window remains, watching a sunrise with nobody standing behind it. Is that window a soul, a brain process, or simply what awareness feels like without its furniture? DMT cannot settle that argument. It may only remove the furniture faster than language can replace it. The mind then rushes to decorate the emptiness with gods, machines, ancestors, and impossible beings. Perhaps those figures are visitors. Perhaps they are shadows cast when awareness meets its own depth. Either way, the witness itself never appears as an object in the room. Music can fill a cathedral without becoming another instrument on the stage. Then ordinary consciousness returns, rebuilding the walls, hanging your name back above the door. You feel as though the witness returned with you. But perhaps it was the one thing that never traveled. If your face can disappear while seeing remains, which one have you been calling yourself?
