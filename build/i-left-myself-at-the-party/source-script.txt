I want to tell you about a Thursday that wasn't a big deal. I decided in the driveway to be easy. That was the whole plan. Be easy tonight. Within ten minutes someone said something I didn't agree with. And I nodded. Not a lie exactly. A nod isn't a sentence. That's what I told myself. Then somebody made a joke that landed on someone who wasn't there to hear it. And I laughed. A beat late, but I laughed. Someone asked what I thought. I said, whatever works. I had a thought. It was right there. I chose easy instead. Nobody bullied me. That's the part I can't dress up. The room was fine. The people were fine. I was the one who left. I stayed three more hours and I was pleasant the entire time. Everybody had a nice time, including me. That's true too. Then I got in the car. And the car was very quiet. That's when it comes. Not during. Never during. The bill for leaving yourself always arrives the moment you're finally alone. And I sat in my own driveway and understood that I'd left somebody at that party. Not dramatically. He just didn't get counted. Nobody took his coat. I've done that hundreds of times. This is only the one I noticed. And here's what I want to be careful about, because this could turn into self-hatred fast. It isn't a character flaw. It's a habit with very good manners. The fix isn't becoming difficult. I don't want to be difficult. It's saying one true sentence before I leave a room. One. Out loud. I tried it two weeks later. It was awkward and my voice was wrong and nobody minded at all. So the question I'd hand you is smaller than a resolution. What's one true thing you didn't say last time, and who would you actually have lost?
