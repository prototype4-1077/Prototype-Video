Have you tried thinking positive? Wow. Thank you. Apparently my nervous system just needed a motivational quote over a sunset. Problem solved. Nothing calms financial anxiety like whispering, “I am abundant,” while your banking app says, “Let’s not get carried away.” Can my new mindset pay the electric bill? Does the power company accept good vibes? Can I pay for groceries with a vision board? It says I’m spiritually wealthy. And yes, gratitude is important. I’m grateful for my health, my home, and the bill collector who keeps checking on me. Survival mode isn’t a bad attitude. It’s your brain running forty-seven tabs. One is playing an alarm, and you can’t find it. Then a productivity influencer appears: “Wake up at five, journal, and dominate the day!” Sir, I am awake at five. I’m staring at the ceiling, calculating whether I can afford cheese. By 5:30, I’ve prepared for seven disasters that haven’t happened. The day has been thoroughly dominated. Positive thinking can help—but it cannot replace safety. You cannot affirmation your way out of exhaustion or raise your vibration high enough that your landlord forgets the rent. And you cannot heal your nervous system by screaming, “I AM CALM!” That’s not calm. That’s a hostage situation. Getting out of survival mode requires painfully boring things: rest, stability, support, boundaries, and an actual meal. It means solving one problem today—not repairing your childhood and entire personality before lunch. It might mean asking for help. We’d rather collapse in the kitchen than inconvenience another human. Your nervous system doesn’t need another speech about becoming unstoppable. It needs evidence that stopping is finally safe. So yes, think positively. But also sleep, eat something, and ask for help. Stop following people who think your nervous breakdown is a time-management problem. Sometimes you don’t need a better morning routine. Sometimes you need money, support, and a nap.
