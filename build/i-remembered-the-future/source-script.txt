The clock softened. Its numbers poured from its face and gathered on the floor like silver rain. One second stretched until it held weather, distance, and its own horizon. The present split open. Behind it stood every past moment and every moment I had not reached. They were nested, like dreams sleeping inside dreams that would someday remember them. A tree rose before me. Every branch was a life I could become; every leaf held another face. I saw myself wealthy and hollow. Poor and radiant. Loved by crowds. Unknown and finally honest. None felt imaginary. Each looked back with the wounded dignity of someone I had abandoned. I asked which was real. All were real as possibility. None were promised. The future was not a destination. It was weather gathering around every choice. A word became wind. Kindness changed the temperature. Fear darkened an entire season. Every decision rang like a tuning fork. Distant versions of me trembled in response. Then time became a prism. One beam entered; a thousand lives emerged, each claiming to be the original light. I saw my death through several doors. Each opened into the same impossible brightness. I saw my birth as eternity narrowing itself enough to become touchable. My life turned inside out. Memory was the shadow of a much larger shape. Something looked backward through me. The future remembered me before I became it. Déjà vu was a message sent upstream, a ripple reaching the stone before impact. I entered a library where books wrote themselves from the final page toward the first. My life was one book. I saw every page, yet still had to turn them one by one. Eternity stopped feeling like endless time. It became the page holding every moment. If the page existed, did choice matter? The answer was deeply human. Choice was not how I escaped infinity. Choice was how infinity acquired texture, consequence, and a point of view. The present became a needle, drawing one thread through countless possible worlds. Every clock opened like an eye. Their hands became wings, and the future rushed through me in both directions. I returned to the room, but tomorrow felt attentive, as though the future listened to how I became it.
