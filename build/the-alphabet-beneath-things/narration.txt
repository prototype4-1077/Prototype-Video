To explain consciousness, I would call it a house whose rooms move whenever attention turns the handle. Usually, I stand in one room and mistake its furniture for the whole building. Attention widens, narrows, turns inward, and quietly changes what feels real. I can vanish into a book while my body waits in a chair, loyal as a coat. When I return, the room remains. My spotlight simply stopped feeding it light. Different states of consciousness are different rooms receiving electricity. Every room speaks in symbols because raw feeling is too large for language unless it folds itself. Joy can repaint a street. Grief can lower winter over a kitchen in July. The world did not swear allegiance to my mood. Mood became the glass through which it arrived. A symbol is that glass made portable. It carries more than physical weight. A key is metal, permission, escape, or return, depending on which part of me holds it. The object stays still. Meaning circles it like weather around a mountain. Fire can be warmth, danger, desire, purification, or anger's red unfinished sentence. My private alphabet is built from childhood rooms, songs, smells, scars, and slamming doors. Someone sees an old coat. I feel a hand from a winter that no longer exists. Symbols cannot be translated like receipts. The same image spends differently in every life. In dreams, one doorway can be birth, funeral, warning, and forgiveness at once. Waking thought seats one meaning per chair. Dreaming lets the whole audience speak. The more exact a symbol becomes, the less sky it carries. Precision can trim off stars. Even blankness may work. A pause can be language backstage, changing costumes. Nothing may be the workshop where tomorrow's image receives a handle. Multiple focus means I may exceed the room my waking mind rents. Body, dream, art, and ritual may be alphabets writing one living sentence. Maybe I am a breathing doorway through which larger awareness briefly learns my name. Now I look at one ordinary object and ask what invisible story it carries for me.
