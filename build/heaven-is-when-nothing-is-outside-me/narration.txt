Okay. Let me review the situation. I have spent an impressive amount of energy believing I am a sealed container with good hair. A private island of consciousness. Completely separate. Entirely alone. Somehow still offended that nobody understands me. Very efficient. Build walls around myself, then complain that no one visits. Maybe hell is not a furnace beneath the earth. Maybe hell is the moment I look at every other person and see only an intruder. A universe of locked doors, each one whispering, "Whatever is behind me cannot possibly belong to you." In that kind of hell, even love feels suspicious. Kindness must be hiding an invoice. Vulnerability is just danger that learned emotional vocabulary. And every stranger becomes another country preparing for war. But what if heaven is not somewhere I go? What if heaven begins when the walls become windows? Not when I lose myself, but when I realize the self was never the prison. The prison was the belief that everything outside my skin was foreign. Maybe consciousness is not a collection of candles scattered through the dark. Maybe it is one fire appearing through billions of separate flames. Each flame has its own shape. Its own dance. Its own magnificent little opinion about the wind. But none of them invented the fire. And no, this does not mean I must trust everyone, tolerate cruelty, or invite chaos over for dinner. Apparently becoming spiritually awake does not require becoming spiritually gullible. A door can close without turning the whole universe into an enemy. A boundary can say, "You may not enter," without saying, "You are not part of life." That distinction might be the first window. Maybe every act of compassion is reality remembering itself. Every moment I truly see another person, one brick disappears from the invisible wall between us. Not because we become identical. A choir does not create harmony by forcing every voice onto the same note. Heaven may be difference without exile. Connection without possession. Love without the demand to disappear inside someone else. So perhaps I am not trapped in a universe filled with strangers. Perhaps I am standing inside one immeasurable house, meeting consciousness through different windows. Some are bright. Some are broken. Some are closed because the room behind them is still healing. But the same night leans against every pane. The same dawn is trying to enter. And maybe I am not alone. Maybe I have simply been talking to myself through unfamiliar faces.
