The strangest part of some DMT experiences is not that everything feels alien. It is that the impossible can feel more familiar than your own kitchen. A room you have never entered seems remembered. A presence you cannot name feels disappointed that you are late. That feeling proves nothing about another world. Altered brains can manufacture certainty as easily as images. But the metaphor it leaves behind is difficult to shake. Imagine consciousness as a building that has spent its entire life believing it invented its own rooms. Every memory is a hallway. Every belief is a wall. Every habit is a staircase worn smooth by repetition. Then DMT tears away the drywall for several minutes. Behind the personality, you glimpse repeating structures that seem older than the person using them. The geometry does not merely surround you. It behaves as if it knows where your thoughts will turn. The beings may feel intelligent because the mind gives a face to whatever organizes the mind. Not necessarily aliens, then, but functions wearing ceremonial masks. A gatekeeper could be the sensation of crossing a boundary. A teacher could be understanding given eyes. A surgeon could be change translated into hands, instruments, and unbearable precision. The experience becomes architecture explaining itself through characters. A blueprint does not resemble the finished building, yet every doorway secretly obeys it. A snowflake does not remember a previous winter. It remembers an equation by becoming its shape. Perhaps that is why the encounter can feel like memory without an event to remember. The pattern is not recalling a place. It is recognizing one of its own expressions. If consciousness is assembled from rules deeper than personality, meeting those rules might feel like meeting ancestors. Not ancestors in history, but ancestors in structure: the grammar that existed before the sentence called you. You return without proof, coordinates, or a photograph that survives the crossing. Only the suspicion that identity is a renovation built inside something immeasurably older. When the blueprint recognizes the building, which one is doing the remembering?
