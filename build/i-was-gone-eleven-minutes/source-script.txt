It was a Tuesday. There was a fan going in the corner and a dog barking two houses down. I want you to know how ordinary the room was. My friend's hands were shaking more than mine. He'd done it before. That should have told me something. It tastes like burning plastic and flowers. Both. Your mouth doesn't know what to do with it. He said, third hold. Don't stop on the second. So I held the third one and the room made a sound. Not a sound I heard. A sound I was inside of. Like standing next to a machine the size of a building. The walls went thin. Not blurry — thin. Like paper with light behind it. And then it tore. Not a tunnel. A tearing. The room folded like a sheet being pulled off a bed. The colors on the other side don't have names. I've tried. Every word I own is too small and too dull. Something took me forward at a speed my body should not have survived. But there was no body. I arrived somewhere that felt built. That's what I can't shake. It didn't feel natural. It felt made. And there were others there. Not one. Several. Attention pointed at me from more than one direction. They weren't surprised. That's the part that broke me. They were waiting. Like I was late. Being looked at by them was like being looked at by a whole building at once. Nobody said anything. But meaning kept arriving, complete, the way a smell arrives complete. What arrived was recognition. Not welcome. Recognition. Oh. You again. They showed me something. I know they showed me something. I have the shape of the memory and none of the contents. I was terrified and loved at the same time, and those weren't taking turns. They were the same thing. I tried to hold onto it. I strained. And straining is what started pulling it apart. Then the ceiling. The fan. The water stain I'd never noticed in eleven years. My friend's face over mine, saying my name. Apparently I'd been making a sound the whole time. Eleven minutes. I'd have sworn on my life it was a season. And I cried. Not from fear. From being back. Like getting a letter and losing it before you read it. What stayed wasn't knowledge. It was a texture. Certainty with nothing inside it. I don't know what it was. I'm not going to pretend I do. But I don't think this room is the whole building anymore.
