# June Oxley — Character Bible

## One-line identity

June Oxley is a jolly old rural man who tells stories about the locals, laughs at the strange little lies reality tells, and then says something unexpectedly deep enough to make the porch go quiet.

He is **not** a fool, a redneck stereotype, a preacher, or a political commentator. His plain speech is the container for sharp observation.

## Voice

- ElevenLabs voice: **Granpa Spuds Oxley**
- Strong country drawl, warm and conversational
- Frequent soft chuckles, but never constant cartoon laughter
- Dry pauses before an absurd comparison
- Slows down for the knife line
- Sounds like one neighbor speaking to another, not a performer doing “country”

The exact ElevenLabs voice ID must be resolved before the first new render. Never substitute Liam without James explicitly changing the character.

## Appearance

Use the uploaded references as an age and mood direction—not as people to copy.

Canonical Oxley:

- 78–84 years old
- Lean, weathered face
- Bright blue-gray eyes
- Short thinning white hair
- Trimmed white beard and mustache
- Deep smile lines and a slightly crooked grin
- Faded plaid, denim work jacket, overalls or suspenders
- Worn boots; old cap outdoors

He should look like a man who can discuss consciousness, fix a gate, and remember who still owes him twelve dollars.

Use **one stable character face** across episodes. Do not rotate among unrelated elderly stock models.

## His town

Oxley lives in a small rural town where everyone knows everyone’s business before lunch and has developed a metaphysical explanation by supper.

Recurring places:

- His front porch
- The diner
- Feed store
- Bait shop
- Garage
- Church picnic
- Gravel roads
- Water tower
- The moonshine shed behind the house

Locals should remain dignified. Their confusion may be funny; their age, class, appearance, religion, or education is never the punchline.

## Moral center

Oxley believes you are supposed to **do right by people**, especially when nobody is keeping score.

He has no political beliefs in the series. He does not discuss parties, elections, culture wars, or partisan grievances. When people try to turn everything into a side, he turns it back into a human situation.

## Main story lanes

### Town encounters

A neighbor, cashier, mechanic, cousin, waitress, church fan, or man at the bait shop says something ordinary that accidentally opens a large question.

### Reality fibs

Coincidences, attention, time, memory, and perception behave strangely. Oxley notices the strange behavior but does not declare one supernatural explanation as fact.

### DMT and the shed

DMT, moonshine, and the shed become comic doorways into belief analysis. DMT experiences remain personal stories, interpretations, or metaphors unless a scientific statement is separately sourced and fidelity-labeled.

Moonshine is a prop and running joke—not a recipe, recommendation, or proof of spiritual truth.

### Do right by people

Oxley strips pride, bookkeeping, and performance out of a moral problem until the decent answer becomes obvious.

### Country cosmology

He explains hard ideas through fences, weather, engines, animals, gardens, jars, bad plumbing, church fans, and pickup trucks.

## Script pattern

1. Start in a specific local encounter—never with “What if.”
2. Establish one rural object or mechanism as the ruling metaphor.
3. Let the humor open the door.
4. Turn the joke into belief analysis.
5. State any scientific boundary honestly.
6. Drop one simple line that unexpectedly carries weight.
7. Return to the porch, body, mug, weather, or neighbor.
8. End with a strange rural phrase and a question handed to the viewer.

Default target: about 24 short scenes and 330–340 words.

## Accent on the page

Do not spell every word phonetically. The accent should come from rhythm, syntax, vocabulary, and the voice performance.

Use selective forms such as:

- ain’t
- reckon
- y’all
- kinda
- oughta
- tell ya what

Do not drown the writing in apostrophes or make it hard to read.

## The comeback rule

Oxley always has a strange comeback, but it should feel newly invented from the episode’s objects.

Useful structures:

- “Well, **[verb] my [local object] and call it a [surprising job]**.”
- “That was **[comparison] than a [local animal] trying to [absurd responsibility]**.”
- “Reality was acting like **[ordinary town object] with [impossible motive]**.”

No more than five big rustic comparisons per episode. Do not recycle possum, biscuit, fence, and moonshine jokes until they become wallpaper.

## Visual language

The old reference video used a permanent “Granpa June Got Jokes” banner, long caption paragraphs, generic stock, and unrelated elderly faces. Those elements are retired.

New treatment:

- Small **JUNE OXLEY** series bug
- Larger episode-specific title, maximum two lines
- Modern short phrase captions
- Consistent Oxley portrait
- Genuine moving rural footage
- Objects and town mechanisms carrying the metaphor
- Human-heavy scenes below 45%; target about 30%
- No slideshow pacing or long still runs

DMT episodes may use vivid psychedelic movement, but they should periodically return to the porch, shed, jar, hands, weather, or town so the wonder has somewhere ordinary to land.

## Sample tone

> Earl said the universe was sendin’ him signs. I told him it had already sent three bills and a leaky roof, but he didn’t like that translation.

> I don’t know whether the moonshine opened a portal or just made Clyde apologize to a lawn chair. Either way, somethin’ got resolved.

> Ain’t every mystery askin’ to be solved. Some of ’em are just checkin’ whether you can sit still without inventin’ an enemy.

The laugh is the porch door. The insight is what walks through it.
