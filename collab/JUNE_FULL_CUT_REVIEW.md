# The Twelve-Dollar Mug — one uninterrupted screening

Watch the 38-second review with sound at normal speed. One timestamp
where the story stops feeling natural is more useful than a checklist.
An independent viewer should be able to say what June decided to do
and why he changed his mind.

Three places to revisit only if they pulled you out of the story:

- **0:00–0:13, setup.** Does June's narration carry the setup, or do the
  held character poses look like someone is speaking without moving?
- **0:18.8–0:22.6, change of heart.** At the first pose June is looking
  out with the pencil raised; at the second his gaze and the pencil
  drop. This is currently a hard change between authored drawings at
  0:20.67, not a continuously acted expression. Does it read as June
  noticing the other man's discomfort before he decides to pour?
- **0:26.30, 0:28.80, 0:30.40, pour and turn to camera.** Do the two
  same-view joins disappear into the pour, and is the later cut to
  June's face motivated? Listen for any click, static or gap in the
  full playback rather than inferring cleanliness from a meter.

Recommended first performance experiment if the turn feels stiff:
animate the gaze dropping before the pencil lowers, keeping the mug,
table and room fixed. The existing start and end art already give a
clear target. If viewers understand the movement but not *why*, a
brief reverse insert of the visitor's hand is a different remedy; it
does not require casting or drawing a second face. Try one change,
assemble it into the full narrative, and compare in context. Do not
touch the passed closing blink, mouth/hand adapters or accepted PCM.

Final voice and whole-film human review remain open even if the build
and technical checks pass. Nothing here grants production promotion.
